# 35 - Assumptions Example: Zirx (On-Demand Valet)

## The Product

**Zirx**: mobile app to hail an on-demand valet who parks your car in cheap storage, then returns it when you request.

**Problem**: Hard to park in big cities. Garages are inconvenient and expensive.

**Solution**: Flexible on-demand valets via mobile app, storing cars in cheaper locations.

## 10 Assumptions Identified

Using the technique: *"The following must be true for this to succeed:"*

### About the Problem
| # | Assumption |
|---|---|
| 1 | Parking is bad/difficult in big cities |
| 2 | People find parking garages inconvenient |
| 3 | People find parking garages expensive |

### About What Matters to the User
| # | Assumption |
|---|---|
| 4 | Drivers care about price |
| 5 | Drivers care about convenience |
| 6 | Drivers don't care much about speed (on-demand may be slower than a garage you already know) |

### About Substitutes
| # | Assumption |
|---|---|
| 7 | People are not satisfied with parking in a garage (the existing alternative) |

### About the Market
| # | Assumption |
|---|---|
| 8 | Enough drivers have smartphones |
| 9 | Drivers can operate the app while driving (solo drivers may struggle) |

### About the Other Side of the Market
| # | Assumption |
|---|---|
| 10 | People want to work as on-demand valets (supply side) |

### About Trust
| # | Assumption |
|---|---|
| 11 | People will be comfortable giving their car to a stranger ("Zirx agent") |

## Next Step

Organize these assumptions **by risk** — which one, if wrong, sinks the whole ship?
