# 66 - Working with Engineers & Designers

## Engineers: Tips

| # | Tip |
|---|---|
| **1** | **If something goes wrong, it's YOUR fault.** You gave them the specs — own it |
| **2** | **Show the future vision** — tell them where the feature is going long-term so they can plan technically |
| **3** | **Do the work upfront** — check commit logs, look up data yourself before asking them. Sign of respect |
| **4** | **Watch for tech debt** — trust their judgment when they say "let's build it right the first time" |
| **5** | **Don't treat them as an agency** — never just hand completed specs to code. Involve them in product discussions |

### Key: Involve Engineers in Product Decisions

- During planning meetings, ask: "What do you think about this from a **product** perspective?"
- They have good ideas — just because they code doesn't mean they can't think about users

### Be Detailed

Engineers need to know edge cases you wouldn't think of:
- What happens in another language?
- What's the error state?
- What happens when there's no data? (empty state)

---

## Designers: Tips

| # | Tip |
|---|---|
| **1** | **Tell them limitations early** — one missing data point can change their entire design |
| **2** | **Don't treat them as "make it pretty" people** — they know more about user interactions than you |
| **3** | **Function as a team** — share data, user feedback, and the reasoning behind decisions |
| **4** | **Never tell them WHAT to change** — highlight the problem broadly, let them figure out the solution |
| **5** | **Talk about user PROBLEMS first, solutions second** — don't say "add photo sharing." Say "users can't express themselves with media in messages" |

### The PM Rule

> You own the **what** and the **why**. Engineers and designers own the **how**.
