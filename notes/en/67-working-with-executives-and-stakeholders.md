# 67 - Working with Executives & Stakeholders

## Executives: The Secret is Brevity

| # | Tip |
|---|---|
| **1** | **Be brief** — 2 bullet points, not 2 paragraphs. They're the busiest people in the company |
| **2** | **Speak in business effect** — not "users can do X more frequently" but "this means +$Y revenue" or "this moves metric Z" |
| **3** | **Match their communication style** — some hate email. Try hallway updates, lunch, or next meeting |

> If they want more detail, they know who to ask — you.

## All Stakeholders (Sales, Legal, Marketing, etc.)

| # | Tip |
|---|---|
| **1** | **Talk to every team as much as possible** — meetings, lunch, any excuse to connect |
| **2** | **Show you know the user base, tech, and business** — builds confidence in your decisions |
| **3** | **Update frequently** — but make clear if it's FYI or needs a reply. Use internal wiki/blog for passive updates |
| **4** | **Tell the story** — when sharing a completed feature, explain why this solution and what alternatives you considered |

## Communication Cheat Sheet

| Audience | Style | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| **Engineers** | Detailed, specific, edge cases | Technical feasibility, future architecture |
| **Designers** | Problems first, creative freedom | User experience, constraints early |
| **Executives** | Brief, bullet points | Business impact, metrics, revenue |
| **Sales** | Business effect, timeline | What they can sell, when it ships |
| **Legal** | Compliance, risk | What data we collect, privacy implications |
| **Marketing** | Positioning, timeline | What's launching, messaging, target audience |
