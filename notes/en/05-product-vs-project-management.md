# 05 - Product vs Project Management

They are **not** the same. But project management is a **skill** every PM needs.

## Core Difference

| | Product Manager | Project Manager |
|---|---|---|
| **Responsible for** | Achieving a **goal/metric** | Completing a **project** |
| **Success means** | Hit the KPI (e.g., +10% conversion) | Deliver on **time** and on **budget** |
| **How to get there** | Up to you — research, experiment, iterate | Predefined plan — execute it |
| **Approach** | Flexible, experimental | Rigid, structured |
| **Risk tolerance** | High — try different approaches | Low — follow the plan |

## Examples

| Scenario | Who leads? | Why? |
|---|---|---|
| Increase checkout conversion by 10% | **Product Manager** | Goal-based, method is open |
| Build a skyscraper on time and budget | **Project Manager** | Defined scope, no experimenting |
| Merge two tech platforms after acquisition | **Project Manager** | Known outcome, many moving pieces |
| Agency builds software for a client spec | **Project Manager** | Requirements defined, execute on budget |
| Decide what feature to build next for users | **Product Manager** | Requires research, no predefined answer |

## Where They Overlap

PMs switch to "execution mode" once they move from discovery to development — that's where **project management skills** kick in (timelines, tracking, delivery).

---

## Buzzwords

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **KPI** | Key Performance Indicator — the metric that defines success |
| **Conversion Rate** | % of users who complete a desired action (e.g., checkout) |
| **Execution Mode** | Phase where the team builds what was decided — focus shifts to delivery |
| **Requirements** | Defined specs of what needs to be built |
