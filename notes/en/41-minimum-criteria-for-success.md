# 41 - Minimum Criteria for Success (MVP Step 4)

## Three Outcomes of Any MVP Test

| Outcome | What to do |
|---|---|
| **1. Categorically false** | Dump it — easy call |
| **2. Clearly true** | Proceed — easy call |
| **3. Somewhere in the middle** | **90% of the time you'll be here** — this is why you need MCS |

## Why You Need MCS (Minimum Criteria for Success)

- Without it, you can't tell if you **passed or failed**
- A validated hypothesis ≠ a clear signal to proceed (could be validated but not worth the investment)
- Gives experiments **clarity and meaning** — not "improve stuff" but "improve stuff by X amount"

## The Two-Sided Calculation

```
| COST (left)              | EXPECTED REWARD (right)    |
|--------------------------|----------------------------|
| Developer time           | Target metric improvement   |
| Your time + team time    | Conversion rate             |
| Cash spent               | Engagement (likes, shares)  |
| Promotion/ad costs       | Sign-up percentage          |
| Opportunity cost         | Revenue per customer        |
| Brand risk               | Positive reaction rate      |
| Displacement of other    |                             |
| initiatives              |                             |
```

## Right Side: Expected Reward Metrics

| For new products | For existing products |
|---|---|
| % of people signing up | Time spent on page |
| Conversion rate | Actions per session |
| Open rate (email) | Number of shares/likes |
| % positive reactions | Revenue per user |
| Revenue per customer | Retention/churn |

> Every metric must be **directly or indirectly related** to a crucial business metric. Otherwise you're inflating **vanity metrics**.

## Left Side: Costs to Consider

- Developer/designer/PM **time** (time = money)
- **Cash** for tools, contractors, promotion
- **Opportunity cost** — if you pursue X and fail, you could have done Y
- **Displacement** — the email/ad slot you used could have gone elsewhere
- **Brand risk** — especially at large companies
- **Morale** — team impact of pursuing and failing

## Factor in Org Size

| Small org | Large org |
|---|---|
| Scrappy experiments OK | Need something meatier — they put their name on it |
| Lower brand risk | Higher brand considerations |
| Tighter resources | More resources but higher opportunity cost |
