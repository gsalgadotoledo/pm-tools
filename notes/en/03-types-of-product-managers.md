# 03 - Types of Product Managers

## The Three Main Types

### 1. Internal Product Manager

- Part of an "internal tools" team.
- Builds tools **for people within their own company**, not for external users.
- Example: software the support team uses to reset passwords or change account info.
- **Stakeholders**: internal employees who use those tools.

### 2. Business-to-Business (B2B) Product Manager

- Also called **SaaS Product Manager** (Software as a Service).
- Builds products whose **clients are other companies** (e.g., Oracle, Salesforce).
- Works closely with **sales teams** to ensure the product meets business requirements of client companies.
- **Stakeholders**: the businesses buying the product + internal sales teams.

### 3. Business-to-Consumer (B2C) Product Manager

- The most common type.
- Builds products for the **general public** (e.g., Facebook, Twitter, Instagram, SoundCloud).
- Requires a wide range of skills, **vision**, and **creativity**.
- Focuses on maximizing **usage metrics** — no one tells you exactly what to build next.
- Spends a lot of time talking to users, creating prototypes, and analyzing data.
- **Stakeholders**: millions of end users.

---

## B2B vs B2C — Key Difference

| | B2B | B2C |
|---|---|---|
| **Who decides what to build?** | Sales says "we'll make $5M if we build X" | No one can predict exactly what to build |
| **Validation method** | Business requirements from clients | User testing, prototyping, data analysis |
| **Stakeholder proximity** | Direct contact via sales | Indirect — millions of anonymous users |

---

## Buzzwords

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Stakeholders** | People you are building for, or who have input into what you are building |
| **Internal Tools** | Software built for use within the company, not for external customers |
| **B2B** | Business-to-Business — selling products to other companies |
| **B2C** | Business-to-Consumer — building products for the general public |
| **SaaS** | Software as a Service — software delivered as a subscription service to businesses |

---

## Tools & Techniques

| Tool/Technique | Context |
|---|---|
| **Prototyping** | B2C PMs create multiple prototypes to validate ideas before building |
| **User Testing** | Testing the product directly with real users to gather feedback |
| **A/B Testing** | Comparing two versions of a feature to see which performs better with users |
| **Data/Metrics Analysis** | Using quantitative data to decide what to build — the bigger the user base, the more powerful the testing |
