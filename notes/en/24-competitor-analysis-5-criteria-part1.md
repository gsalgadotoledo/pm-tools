## 1. Product Core

**What it means**: The quality of the competitor's product/engineering team.

> "Any day, I would take one great engineer over a hundred average engineers." — Mark Zuckerberg

**Example**: Instagram had only 13 employees (8 engineers) when Facebook bought them for $1 billion.

| Their product core vs yours | What it means for you |
|---|---|
| **Better than yours** | They can out-execute you — avoid competing on engineering quality |
| **Worse than yours** | Wade into areas that rely on engineering quality — they'll struggle to imitate |

## 2. User Base Size

**What it means**: A large user base gives inherent advantages that have nothing to do with product quality.

| Advantage | Why |
|---|---|
| **Market domination on launch** | Launch any new feature and instantly bring millions into it |
| **Easier press coverage** | Media covers larger, well-known companies more |
| **Better deal negotiation** | "I'm bringing X million users to the table" |
| **Intrinsic network value** | Social products are only as valuable as their user count |

**Example: Apple Music vs Spotify**

| | Spotify + SoundCloud | Apple |
|---|---|---|
| **Users** | ~30M paying combined | 1 billion |
| **Late to market?** | No | Yes |
| **Does it matter?** | — | No — 1% conversion = instant serious competitor |

> In this case, size does matter. A massive user base can overcome being late to market.
