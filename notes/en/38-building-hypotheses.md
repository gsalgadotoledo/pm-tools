# 38 - Building Hypotheses (MVP Step 3)

## Why Hypotheses?

Assumptions are **raw and vague** — you can't build a test around "people are not satisfied with parking." You need to get specific:
- **Who** exactly is unsatisfied?
- **How** unsatisfied are they?
- **Why** do you think they're unsatisfied?

## Assumption → Hypothesis

| | Assumption | Hypothesis |
|---|---|---|
| **Format** | Rough belief | Single, written, testable statement |
| **Precision** | Vague | Specific (who, what, how much) |
| **Actionable?** | No | Yes — you can design a test around it |

## Example

| Assumption | Hypothesis |
|---|---|
| "People are not satisfied with parking in garages" | "Downtown drivers aged 25-40 going out on Friday nights find parking garages inconvenient enough to pay $15+ for an alternative" |

## Why "MVP Experiment" Not Just "MVP"

This is a **science experiment**:
- Identify assumptions → Build hypothesis → Test → Prove/disprove
- Brings **clarity** to you and your team
- Prevents forgetting what you're actually trying to validate

## Strategy: Two Approaches

| Approach | When to use |
|---|---|
| **One hypothesis per assumption** | When assumptions are independent and need separate validation |
| **One hypothesis that covers multiple assumptions** | When proving one thing true can rule out several assumptions at once — "killing multiple birds with one MVP stone" |
