# 27 - Building Feature Tables in Practice

## Setup

### Columns (top): Competitors

- Start with **Direct Competitors** (4-5 minimum)
- Put Indirect, Substitute, Potential in a **separate table** — analyze after

| | You | Direct 1 | Direct 2 | Direct 3 | Direct 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

### Rows (left): Features & Factors

Put what **your target customer cares about**, not what competitors think is important.

| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Factors** | Price, reliability, uptime, ease of use, design |
| **Features** | "Does X", "ability to do Y" |
| **Restaurant example** | Prix fixe 3-course meal, online reservations |

> Your product vision is your **bet** on how to win the market. You won't beat every competitor in every category.

## How to Fill It In

1. **Go use their products** — give each an honest appraisal
2. Keep answers **short and uniform**: Yes/No + 1-2 words if needed
3. Make it **glanceable** — don't write paragraphs in cells

## Which Table Matters Most?

| Table | Goal |
|---|---|
| **Direct competitors** | Stay up to date — watch what they add, remove, and how they'll react to you |
| **Indirect/Substitute/Potential** | Make sure it's **obvious** you're better for your target customer |

> If you're not obviously better than a **substitute**, you have a fundamental problem. Go back to scratch.

## Template

```
|                        | You    | Comp A | Comp B | Comp C | Comp D |
|------------------------|--------|--------|--------|--------|--------|
| Price                  | $X     | $X     | $X     | $X     | $X     |
| Feature 1              | Yes    | Yes    | No     | Yes    | No     |
| Feature 2              | Yes    | No     | Yes    | No     | Yes    |
| Ability to do X        | Yes    | No     | No     | Yes    | No     |
| Ease of use            | High   | Med    | High   | Low    | Med    |
| [your key factor]      | ...    | ...    | ...    | ...    | ...    |
```
