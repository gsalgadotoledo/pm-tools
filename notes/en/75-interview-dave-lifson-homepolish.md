# 75 - Interview: Dave Lifson (VP Product, Homepolish)

## His Background
Amazon (dev → PM on recommendations) → Etsy (first Head of Product, employee ~50) → own startup → General Assembly (employee 25, grew to 400) → Poppin → Homepolish

## Key Insights

### What is Product Management?
> "Deep understanding of the customer + deep understanding of the business → marry those two to identify opportunities, prioritize them, and devise the right solution for the right time."

> "We don't create anything. We create the conditions for everyone else to produce higher quality, more efficient, more effective work."

### War Story: General Assembly "Front Row"
- Founders wanted a subscription product to scale beyond classrooms (no customer problem identified — came from a spreadsheet)
- 3-month deadline → no time for proper research
- Packaged existing video library as $25/month subscription
- LTV never worked out. **Subscription is an odd model for education.**
- Lesson: "As a PM, I should have recognized immediately there was no mention of the customer."

### How to Get Into PM Without Experience

> "Show that you understand the **PM process** and can apply it to different situations."

The process:
1. Identify the problem
2. Identify the customer
3. Propose a solution
4. Iterate to product-market fit
5. Validate assumptions with different MVP tools

Ways to demonstrate this:
- **Hackathons** — apply the process in 2 days
- **Internal involvement** — even from marketing/analyst roles, join user research or opportunity analysis
- **Structure your resume** to highlight each step — hiring managers will see you "get it" even without the PM title

### If You're an Account Manager (or Similar)
> "Put together what you think the **product roadmap** should look like. Support your proposal with data, user research, and the risks/assumptions you'd validate."

### Hard Skills to Learn

| Skill | Priority | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| **Agile basics** | Must | User stories, sprint planning, retros — the mechanics |
| **Wireframing** | Must | Pen and paper is fine — communicate what's in your head through pictures |
| **How the internet works** | Must | Type a URL → what are the 10-15 steps before the page appears? Go deep |
| **SQL** | Extra credit | PMs often need data and there's no data department to get it for them |
| **Programming language** | Not needed | Don't need HTML/CSS/Ruby — understand architecture instead |

### What Hiring Managers Actually Look For
Not the PM title — but evidence you've done the **steps of the process** in past roles.
