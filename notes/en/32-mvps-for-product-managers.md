# 32 - MVPs for Product Managers

## Startups vs Established Companies

| | Startup | Established Company |
|---|---|---|
| **Risk tolerance** | Low — failed product = company dies | High — failed product barely dents the balance sheet |
| **Resources** | Scarce — time, money, people | Abundant — cushion exists |
| **MVP focus** | Don't waste resources (time, money) | Don't miss opportunities (brand, opportunity cost) |
| **If MVP fails** | Company may die | You survive to live another day |
| **PM title** | Probably "co-founder" | Product Manager |

## Risk Tolerance Scales with Company Size

```
Risk Tolerance
  ^
  |                        /
  |                      /
  |                   /
  |                /
  |            /
  |        /  ← Concerns shift from "saving resources"
  |     /       to "brand" and "opportunity cost"
  |  /
  +---------------------------------> Company Size
```

## What Changes at Larger Orgs

| Startup cares about | Large org cares about |
|---|---|
| Time | **Brand** — huge inherent value to protect |
| Money | **Opportunity cost** — missing a trend or platform is worse than wasting money |
| Survival | **Not being first** to a new platform or market |

## Key Insight: Failed Products Can Still Benefit You

**Example: Google Glass**
- Product failed commercially
- But: barely dented Google's balance sheet
- Generated massive buzz and press
- Enhanced brand as innovative and "swinging for the fences"
- Technology learnings absorbed by other parts of the org

> As a PM, you don't have to think like a scrappy 2-person startup. Failed experiments can benefit your brand, generate learnings, and feed other product lines.
