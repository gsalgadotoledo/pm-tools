# 45 - MVP Types (Part 2): Explainer Video & Fake Landing Page

## 4. Explainer MVP

Fake a video showing your product working — before it's built.

### Two Styles

| Style | How it works | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Tutorial** | Screencast pretending to use the feature. Edit to make static pages look connected and functional | **Dropbox** — founder made a fake demo video on YouTube, got huge response, then built it |
| **Sales/Promo** | Animated or talking-head video pitching the product with CGI/animations | **Framework** — pitched new features with fancy animations, features didn't exist yet, "sign up for launch notification" |

**Why it works**: Real products have promo/tutorial videos. Users can't tell the difference.

## 5. Fake Landing Page / Pitch Experiment MVP

One page that pitches your product + a call to action (sign up, buy, etc.).

### Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Landing Page** | Single focused webpage — user can only do one thing (sign up, buy, etc.) |
| **Pitch Experiment** | Online experiment where you pitch a product/feature and observe reactions |

### Why Landing Pages Work as MVPs

- Completely focused — user can't ignore the pitch
- Drive traffic via ads, email, push notifications
- **Gold standard** for entrepreneurial MVP tests

### Examples

| Company | What they did |
|---|---|
| **Buffer** | Created fake page pitching their social media scheduling service. Drove traffic. Watched what people interacted with to build the actual product like a puzzle |
| **Basecamp** (37 Signals) | Uses landing pages for every new side project — "This is what it does, here's why it's awesome, buy it or sign up" |

### For PMs vs Entrepreneurs

| | Entrepreneur | Product Manager |
|---|---|---|
| **Traffic source** | External (ads, social) | Internal (email, push notifications, in-app) |
