# 69 - Preparing for a PM Job

> New chapter: Career in Product Management

## The PM Job Paradox

- **Hard** to get the first job (requires experience)
- **Easy** to switch jobs once you have it (low supply of qualified PMs, high demand)
- No college major for PM — you can't get a degree in it

## Two Ways to Get Experience

### 1. Find PM Experience in Your Current Job

You're probably **already doing PM work** without knowing it. Write down experience with:

| Experience | Why it's PM-relevant |
|---|---|
| **Prioritizing multiple tasks** with a team based on information | Core PM skill — prioritization |
| **Talking to users** of a product (focus groups, customer service) | User research |
| **Reporting bugs** or giving product feedback | QA, product sense |
| **Working with the product team** — sharing data, giving feedback | Cross-functional collaboration |
| **Working across teams** (legal, engineering, sales, marketing) | Communication hub — the PM's main role |
| **Improving a process** — making things more efficient | Process optimization |

### 2. Side Project (next note)

If you can't find PM-relevant experience at your current job.

## What to Do With These Experiences

1. **Detail each one** — write down what you did, why, and the result
2. **Put them on your resume** framed as PM skills
3. **Prepare to discuss them** in PM interviews

## Networking

- Email PMs you find online — ask if you can ask them a few questions
- Browse **Product Hunt**, **LinkedIn**, **Twitter** for PMs in your target industry
- Most PMs are happy to talk — the community is supportive
