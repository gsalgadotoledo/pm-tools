# 39 - Hypothesis Formulas

## What Makes a Hypothesis Actionable?

A hypothesis needs **specifics**: target group, expected action, expected outcome, and optionally why.

## Three Formulas (Simple → Complex)

### Formula 1: Basic (New Products / Entrepreneurs)

> **We believe** [target group] **will** [expected action] **because** [reason].

*Example*: We believe women ages 40-45 will sign up for weekly event newsletters because they want to stay informed about their city.

**Use when**: Establishing basic demand. Looking for a heartbeat of interest.

### Formula 2: With Action (Exploring Changes)

> **If we** [action we take], **we believe** [target group] **will** [expected action] **because** [reason].

*Example*: If we change the buy button color, we believe all users will buy more because they get distracted and often miss it.

**Use when**: Speculating on changes to user behavior. Exploratory.

### Formula 3: With Metrics (Product Managers)

> **We believe** [target group] **has a** [problem] **because** [reason]. **If we** [action], **this** [metric] **will be affected.**

*Example*: We believe mobile shoppers have a low checkout rate because the buy button is hard to see. If we change the button color to red, checkout conversion will increase.

**Use when**: Managing an existing product. Most specific and actionable.

## Summary

| Formula | Complexity | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **1. We believe [who] will [what] because [why]** | Simple | New products, entrepreneurs, basic demand validation |
| **2. If we [do X], we believe [who] will [what] because [why]** | Medium | Exploring behavior changes |
| **3. We believe [who] has [problem] because [why]. If we [do X], [metric] will change.** | Full | Product managers — metrics-driven, most actionable |

## On Problems in Hypotheses

All products solve a problem (even Google Glass solves "reaching for your phone"). If you can't identify the problem, describe a **benefit** instead: "[group] would benefit by [X]."
