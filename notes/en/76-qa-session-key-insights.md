# 76 - Q&A Session: Key Insights

## PM in Service Businesses / Agencies?

- **Historically no** — agencies do project management (fixed scope, budget, deadline)
- **New trend**: Agencies now offer maintenance + iteration → hiring PMs to manage products for clients
- Don Draper is NOT a product manager

## Product Teams ≠ Team of PMs

A **product team** = 1 PM + engineers + designers + (maybe data scientist). Also called a **feature team**.

**Hierarchy at large companies:**
```
VP of Product
  └── Director of Product (e.g., Newsfeed)
       ├── PM: Video in feed + their team
       ├── PM: Ranking algorithm + their team
       └── PM: Ads in newsfeed + their team
```

## Disagreements Between PMs

- A good director **never says "no"** — they ask questions that lead you to the right conclusion
- Company goals (e.g., "increase retention by 5%") narrow the window of disagreement
- Your job is to prove decisions with **data** — if you're guessing, you're not doing your job

## B2B vs B2C: What's "Harder"?

| Type | Pressure type |
|---|---|
| **B2C** | Uncertain, creative pressure — building things users don't know they want yet |
| **B2B** | Sales deadlines, complex corporate hierarchies, customer ≠ user |
| **Internal** | Technology limitations, budget constraints, internal approval hierarchies |

None is "easier" — they have **different types of pressure**.

## How Much Time is Listening?

> **~60% of PM work is listening and communicating.** The rest is wireframes, specs, analysis.

You are a professional information synthesizer:
1. Absorb info from sales, marketing, executives, other PMs, metrics
2. Synthesize: what does this mean for our team?
3. Communicate back out to your team AND other teams

## Never Remove Yourself from User Feedback

Even at scale (Facebook, Google), PMs must personally talk to users:
- Google: ML systems cluster feedback, but PMs still need face-to-face
- Twitter: PM tweets at users directly for feedback
- **Always mix** aggregated data with direct user conversations

## The Casual User Problem (Twitter Example)

- Twitter saturated its "power user" market
- Needs casual users, but casual users don't send feedback
- Solution: algorithmic timeline ranking (show best content first, like Facebook did)
- Challenge: don't anger core users who want chronological feeds

## Sprint Planning: PM's Role

1. **Retrospective** on last sprint
2. **Set expectations** for this sprint
3. **Preview** next 2-3 sprints (bring data, user interviews, wireframes)
4. **Ask engineers**: "Is this feasible? What do you think?"
5. **Never treat engineers as an agency** — involve them in product decisions

## Learn SQL?

> **Yes.** Not mandatory day one, but hugely beneficial. Takes load off data scientists and engineers when you can get data yourself.
