# 08 - Product Lifecycle: Real Examples

## Examples by Phase

| Phase | Example | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Introduction** | Dreem by Rythm (sleep wearable) | Not fully launched, "Apply Now" for early adopters, first version coming soon |
| **Growth** | Snapchat | Only a few years old, Google Trends interest skyrocketing, beginning to make money, competitors appearing |
| **Maturity** | Twitter | Growth stagnating (16→16→18→18→19M), plateau in user numbers, product hard to use for mainstream |
| **Decline** | Yahoo | Hedge funds dumping stock, revenue dropping over 5 years, talks of selling parts of the business |

## How to Research Where a Company Is

| Method | What to look for |
|---|---|
| **Google Trends** | Is interest rising, flat, or falling? |
| **Google Finance** | Revenue trends, stock price over 1-5 years |
| **TechCrunch / Product Hunt** | New products = Introduction phase |
| **News articles** | "Growth stagnating", "selling business", "record revenue" |
| **Revenue data** | Growing fast? Plateau? Declining? |

## Key Takeaway

There's no definitive answer — use public data (trends, revenue, news) to **feel out** where a company sits in the lifecycle. Companies constantly try to **avoid decline** by reinventing their product.
