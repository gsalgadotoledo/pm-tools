# 40 - Hypothesis Example: Zirx

## Building the Hypothesis

### Riskiest Assumption
> "People will be okay letting an on-demand valet park their car."

### Using Formula 1 (Basic)

| Component | Value |
|---|---|
| **Who** | Car owners aged 30-50, income $80K+, in cities with bad parking |
| **Expected action** | Let an on-demand valet park their car |
| **Problem (optional)** | Garages are too expensive |

### The Hypothesis

> "We believe car owners aged 30-50 with incomes over $80K in San Francisco will let an on-demand valet park their car because they find garages too expensive."

## One Hypothesis, Multiple Assumptions Covered

If this hypothesis proves true, it also validates:

| Assumption | How it's covered |
|---|---|
| People think garages are too expensive | Built into the "because" |
| People will trust a stranger with their car | They completed the transaction |
| It's not too difficult to use the app while driving | They successfully booked a pickup |

## Two Approaches (Recap)

| Approach | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| **One big test covering multiple assumptions** | Efficient, faster | Less precise on individual assumptions |
| **Separate hypothesis per assumption** | More precise | Takes longer, more resources |

## Why This Works for the Matrix

This assumption was: **High Risk + Low Difficulty** → Do first (per our quadrant system).
