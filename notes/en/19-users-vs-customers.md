# 19 - Users vs Customers

## They Are Not Always the Same Person

| | Customer/Client | User |
|---|---|---|
| **Who** | The person who **pays** | The person who **uses** it daily |
| **Level** | Directors, VPs, decision-makers | Managers, analysts, individual contributors |
| **Feedback type** | **Macro** — what the product should do in general | **Granular** — "where's the button?", "I want to do X" |
| **Example** | Director of Marketing signs $500K check | Social media manager logs in every day |

## Real Example: Mass Relevance

- Product: social media monitoring platform ($200K-$500K)
- **Customer**: Director of Marketing (buys it, never logs in)
- **User**: Social media managers (log in daily, give detailed feedback)
- **Two separate groups → two separate sets of feedback**

## Why This Matters

- If you only listen to the **customer**, you build for the checkbook, not for usability
- If you only listen to the **user**, you miss strategic/business needs
- As a PM, you must balance **both** sets of feedback
- This is especially common in **B2B** product management
