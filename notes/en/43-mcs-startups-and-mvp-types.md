# 43 - MCS for Startups & Introduction to MVP Types

## Pre-Product MCS: How It Differs

| | Established Product (PM) | Pre-Product (Startup) |
|---|---|---|
| **Metrics that matter** | Avg minutes consumed, retention, engagement | **Validation metrics** — does this product deserve to exist? |
| **Right side (reward)** | Behavioral change, metric improvement | Signals of **real interest** |
| **Opportunity cost** | Important — could build something else | Less important — if it fails, you're looking for a new job |
| **Economic focus** | Feature ROI | Overall economic viability (LTV, gross margins) |

## Validation Metrics (Startups)

| Metric | What it signals |
|---|---|
| % of people who sign up | Interest in the product |
| % of people who share your post | Organic enthusiasm |
| Average purchase price | Willingness to pay |
| Email open rate | Engagement with your message |
| Conversion rate | Moving from interest to action |
| Lifetime Value (LTV) | Long-term economic viability |
| Gross margins | Is this sustainable as a business? |

## Transition: Now Pick Your MVP Type

At this point you have:
1. Problem/solution set
2. Assumptions identified and ranked
3. Hypotheses built
4. Minimum criteria for success defined

**Next**: Choose the right MVP experiment format. The key question:

> How much do you need to "fake" that your product exists in order to get the data you need?

The answer determines which MVP type to use.
